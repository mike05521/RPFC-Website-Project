01 · The goal & why

Replace the Cherokee 235 with a new, MOSAIC-era aircraft

Keep flying the 235 for five years, build on the replacement fund we already hold, and sell the 235 in year 5 to close out the $400k — without touching the 235 engine reserves.

Why now

  • Engine at TBO before the goal. The 235 is at 1,984 of 2,000 hrs, with an overhaul est. at ~$60k around Q4 2027. We keep flying the 235 until we sell it in year 5, so the club will fund that overhaul itself (from engine reserves) well before the new aircraft arrives.
  • More aircraft to choose from. FAA MOSAIC rules broaden the field of aircraft the club can consider.
  • Gradual, not sudden. A defined 5-year runway lets dues and rates change gradually rather than through a one-time member assessment.
  • Members prefer the other planes. The 235 is currently the least desirable plane for the majority of members, so replacing it puts club money behind an aircraft members actually want to fly.

04 · Trade-offs

Pros & cons

Both plans reach the goal on paper. The choice is about cost to today’s members versus aircraft access and execution risk.

Plan B · Cap 35

Pros

  • Smaller increases for current members (+$10 dues, +$10/hr)
  • Spreads cost across more members — more equitable per-member burden
  • Cushion vs goal: surplus ≈ $7,579
  • Growth builds a broader base for future aircraft costs
  • New associate members do not fly the 182 and do not have voting rights — full members keep priority on the club’s top aircraft and control of club decisions
  • Lower-cost entry lets new pilots experience the club first; as full members leave, associates have a natural path to pay the difference and upgrade to full membership
  • Monthly dues stay the same for both member types, so full members have no incentive to downgrade to associate

Cons

  • Relies on recruiting to the 35-member cap — $81k at risk if growth stalls
  • More members per aircraft → tighter scheduling and availability
  • Higher utilization means more wear and maintenance

Plan C · Stay at 30

Pros

  • No dependence on recruiting — more predictable cash flow
  • Preserves current aircraft availability and club culture
  • Simpler to administer and forecast

Cons

  • Bigger increases for existing members (+$20 dues, +$20/hr)
  • $81,000 depends on hourly revenue — sensitive if members fly less than ≈27 hrs/yr each (810 club hrs/yr)
  • Higher cost may itself drive turnover (assumes 3/yr)
  • Cushion vs goal: surplus ≈ $5,829

05 · What stays the same

Common to both plans

Whichever path the board picks, these assumptions don’t change.

✈️

Sell the 235 in year 5

We keep flying the 235 until year 5; the ~$130k sale lands then and counts toward the $400k in both plans — it is not cash today.

🔧

Engine reserves kept

235 engine at 1,984 / 2,000 hrs; ~$60k overhaul ~Q4 2027 — likely before the goal is reached. Reserves are not used for the plane.

📡

172 GTN proceeds

The planned GTN upgrade still happens; the $10/mo upgrade reserve is then redirected — ending future upgrades.

📅

5-year horizon

$10k/yr operating transfer, ≈27 flying hrs per pilot / yr × full members (2026 Flight Log), initiation fees (see What-if controls) go to replacement.

Also on the sheet: Plan A

4-year plan that uses engine and upgrade cash toward the aircraft — faster, but leaves no engine reserve ahead of the 235’s TBO.

Also on the sheet: Plan D

$130/mo member assessment that skips the 235 engine overhaul — a flat monthly charge in place of dues and rate changes.

Context: the October Ad Hoc subcommittee materials noted Plan B recommended 6–1 for the upgrade path. That was not a final vote on an aircraft purchase.

06 · For discussion

Open questions for the board

The club hasn’t decided which way to go. These are the questions that separate B from C.

Can we realistically add 5 members?

What is the waitlist / recruiting pipeline, and what happens to Plan B if we land only 2–3?

How price-sensitive are current members?

Would +$20 dues and +$20/hr (Plan C) push turnover above 4/yr or reduce hours flown?

Is ≈27 hrs / pilot / yr a safe assumption?

It is annualized from ~8 months of the 2026 electronic Flight Log. Hourly increases are $47,250 (B, 945 club hrs/yr) and $81,000 (C, 810 hrs/yr) of the raise. Use the avg-hrs stepper to run a downside case (e.g. 23 hrs).

Which aircraft, at what price?

Confirm the $400k target against MOSAIC-era candidates, plus avionics, insurance and tax.

235 sale value in year 5

The plan sells the 235 in year 5, after the ~$60k overhaul (~Q4 2027). Is ~$130k realistic for a freshly overhauled 235 five years out?

Hybrid option?

e.g. moderate growth (+2–3 members) with +$15 increases, or a fallback trigger if recruiting lags.

Suggested next steps

  1. Finance Committee: sensitivity runs on hours flown, member count, and 235 sale price for B and C.
  2. Membership Committee: recruiting pipeline estimate and capacity per aircraft at 35 members.
  3. Aircraft shortlist with all-in acquisition cost to validate the $400k target.
  4. Member input session before any board vote on a funding plan.
